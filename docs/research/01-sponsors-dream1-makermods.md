### B. Dream-1 model (Dream Labs)
- Launch post: https://dreamlabs.ai/research/dream-1/ . Homepage: https://dreamlabs.ai/ . Platform (login required): https://platform.dreamlabs.ai/ . API docs: https://platform.dreamlabs.ai/api-documentation/
- Dream-1 is a **World Action Model**: it jointly predicts future video frames and an action chunk. It descends from **DreamZero** (Feb 2026, https://dreamzero0.github.io/): a 14B video-diffusion model running at 7 Hz that adapted to YAM with 30 minutes of data (per https://www.eesel.ai/blog/dream-labs-dream-1). Dream-1's size is unpublished. Dream Labs raised a $90M seed. Its CEO is Joel Jang (ex-NVIDIA GR00T).
- **Trained on YAM.** On Metal Arm it runs zero-shot through end-effector poses plus client-side IK. Zero-shot score relative to YAM is **80.5% in sim and 70.5% on the real robot**. FaaS (fine-tuning) raises Metal language-following from **76% to 87%** (sim). (launch post)
- **Task categories most relevant to us are its weakest** (YAM, success rate): Pick&place 40%, **Insert&fit 24%**, Push&slide 19%, **Open&close 16%**, Tool use 6%. The progress scores are much higher (42–69%), so it often gets partway. (launch post)
- **Cost:** $3/hr for Dream-1 and $2/hr for **Dream-1-flash** (the faster, lighter model). (launch post)
- **I/O contract** (from the PyPI README for `dreamlabs-client` v1.1.3, https://pypi.org/project/dreamlabs-client/ , and the Dimensional adapter https://github.com/dimensionalOS/dimos/pull/4653):
  - **Inputs:** 3 cameras: `video.<role>` as base64 JPEG, **resized (stretched) to 320x176, quality 85**. The client keeps **9 frames per camera, 1 every 0.2 s (5 Hz), so about 1.8 s of history**. It also takes `state.<part>` float arrays and `prompt` (task text).
  - **Outputs:** **48-step action chunk at 20 Hz = 2.4 s** of motion. (Marketing says "1.6 s per call". The client default `execution_horizon` is **30 rows = 1.5 s** before the next chunk takes over.) Joint chunks are `q_r_chunk`/`q_l_chunk` [48, joints+1], with the gripper normalized 0 = closed, 1 = open. Fine-tunes answer under `per_key` (`action.<part>`, [48, width]).
  - **Latency:** about **250–450 ms** per call through the gateway once warm. A warning fires if the median exceeds 1 s. Above 700 ms median the client switches to **sync** pacing, where the arms hold while waiting. Rate limit: 3 burst, then 1 call per 0.7 s per session.
  - The client does **seam smoothing** for free: a Savitzky-Golay filter plus a 16-row velocity blend between chunks.
  - **Episode/session:** `client.reset_episode()` between attempts. `policy()` again reloads the config. The **prompt cannot change mid-episode**: you must reset the episode to change task text. This matters for subtask sequencing.
  - **Client-side IK options for Metal** (`ik: {"arm":"metal","head":"eef"}`): `keep_out` (box/capsule obstacles, e.g. the rack!), `reach` (2D allowed area), `region` (3D box per arm, can keep links above the table or out of the other arm's half), `solve: "traj"` (smoother). Every one of these is a free safety/recovery lever for the harness.
- **Fine-tuning as a Service (FaaS)** (PyPI README, and docs/fine_tuning.md in the kit):
  - Data: **LeRobot v2.1** dataset with `meta/info.json` and `meta/tasks.jsonl`, `videos/chunk-NNN/<key>/episode.mp4`. The kit writes it for you.
  - Metal datasets must first be run through `tools/add_eef.py --arm metal`, which adds EEF xyz/rot6/grip using Dream's own Metal model and TCP [0.135, 0, 0] m.
  - `tools/finetune.py <dir>-eef --arm metal --name X --suffix X` does validate → upload → process → queue → poll, and prints a model id `dream-1-ft:<uid8>-<suffix>`. It is resumable with `--dataset-id` or `--job-id`.
  - Knobs: `steps` 500–2000 in steps of 100 (default 1000); `learning_rate` 1e-6..1e-4 (default 1e-5); `lr_schedule="cosine"` (recommended for a single task); `base_share` (0.5 default mixes in Dream data, 0.0 trains only on ours); `resume_from=<our model>` continues training on new data; `use_eef`.
  - **Price:** $100 for 500 steps, **$200 for 1000**, $400 for 2000, prepaid credits. "Check with organizers before you queue one."
  - **Time: about 7–8 h for a 1000-step job with 2–3 cameras**, plus queue wait. Then the **first get_action on a new model returns `pool_warming` for 25–50 min ("allow an hour")**. A queued job **cannot be cancelled**. The account has a cap on concurrent jobs. A failed job costs nothing.
  - Multiple folders inside a parent folder count as multiple sessions, so **one fine-tune can learn several tasks/subtasks** (each with its own `--task` text).
  - The kit says: "Queue your job early, **ideally the evening of day one**, and test on day two."
- **Ways to run:** `dreamcli robot run --task "..." [--checkpoint dream-1-ft:...] [--dry-run]`, `dreamcli robot up` (Live View in the browser), or our own loop with `dream.DreamClient` in `~/dream-env` (close dhk-collect and dreamcli first). Dream's docs are also exposed as an **MCP server** (see the platform's Setup API page). There are driver templates for YAM/Metal/Maker at https://platform.dreamlabs.ai/api-documentation/drivers/
- Third-party OSS integration with useful constants and validation code: Dimensional `dimos/imitation/policy/dreamlabs/` (ACTION_HZ=20, HORIZON=48, OBS_WINDOW=9 @ 0.2 s, TCP 0.1347 m on YAM, ±2° overshoot clip) — branch `cc/feat/dreamlabs-dual-yam`.

### C. MakerMods Metal Arm
- Specs (https://www.makermods.ai/metal-arm): 6 joints + parallel-jaw gripper (0–95 mm stroke), **Damiao actuators over CAN** (USB CAN adapter, slcan), 3 kg payload, ±0.1 mm repeatability, 612.5 mm reach, 5.7 kg, 24 V. Joint speed up to 180°/s (J1–J3) and 220°/s (J4–J6). **No brakes**: the arms go limp at the end of a session. $2,499 per arm, $4,999 for a bimanual station.
- It is **not SO-100/101**. It is a distinct CNC-aluminum arm, but it is LeRobot-integrated through the MakerMods fork. Leader options are a Star Arm 102-LD ($199) or another Metal Arm. **At this event, teleop is the Meta Quest** (kit).
- Software: MakerMods Lab, https://github.com/makermods-robotics/makermodslab (web UI covering record, curate, train ACT, deploy, and **built-in DAgger: hand control back to the leader mid-rollout and merge the corrections**; OpenAPI at docs/api/openapi.json). Python/C++ SDK and ROS: https://github.com/makermods-robotics/metal-python-ros . URDF `metal_with_gripper.urdf` (fetched by `scripts/fetch_urdf.py` in the kit).
- https://github.com/makermods-robotics/metal-arm-harness is an "agent is the policy" CLI: `op tip x y z pitch`, `nudge`, `gripper`, a safety envelope with a table-touch floor calibration, and per-move caps. It is useful for **scripted/primitive moves** (e.g. a deterministic re-grasp or reset) without a learned policy.

## Implications for our strategy
1. **Fine-tune timing dominates everything.** 7–8 h training plus up to 1 h warm-up means **2 fine-tune shots at most**: FT#1 queued by about 13:00–14:00 Sat, ready about 22:00; FT#2 queued Sat night with `resume_from` FT#1 plus new and DAgger data, ready Sun morning. **Collect high-quality demos in the first booked slot and queue FT#1 ASAP.** Do not wait for a "perfect" dataset. Unless the organizers have a fast lane, a third iteration is not realistic.
2. **Use the zero-shot base model from minute one** (`dreamcli robot run --dry-run`, then live) to (a) check the station and (b) measure per-subtask zero-shot success. Our harness must work with the base model, because FT#1 will not exist until night.
3. **Decompose into subtask prompts.** The prompt is fixed per episode and `reset_episode()` is cheap, so the harness should run **4 episodes with 4 prompts**: "unplug the USB cable from the rack", "open the rack panel", and so on. Record demos **per subtask, each in its own folder with its own `--task` text**, then train one multi-task fine-tune. Shorter episodes (10–30 s, as the kit recommends) fit the 2.4 s chunk and 1.8 s visual history far better than one 2-minute episode.
4. **Insertion and open/close are Dream-1's weakest categories** (24% and 16% success zero-shot). That is where the fine-tune data should concentrate: more plug-in demos than unplug demos, with varied cable start poses. Consider **scripted primitives** for the final precise insertion if the rules allow it (metal-arm-harness `tip`/`nudge`, or our own IK with the Metal URDF), with Dream-1 doing the coarse approach.
5. **Success checking:** use a VLM (Fireworks) on the overhead plus wrist frames after each subtask, and maybe gripper-state and joint-torque heuristics (a cable in the gripper means grip < 1.0 while stalled). On failure: `reset_episode()` and re-issue the same subtask prompt, or a recovery prompt such as "pick up the dropped cable". Cap retries by time.
6. **Free safety and accuracy levers in the client IK:** `keep_out` boxes for the rack body, `region` boxes to stop the arms crossing or hitting the table, `solve:"traj"`. These cut catastrophic failures, and therefore resets, at zero data cost.
7. **Latency:** keep station-to-gateway round trips under 700 ms so the client stays in async mode. Send frames at or near 320x176 to cut upload. Check `client.last_timing`. Dream-1-flash may be worth it for the motion-heavy subtasks.
8. **DAgger** is explicitly endorsed by the organizers. Plan a Saturday-evening session of base or FT#1 rollouts with Quest takeovers to collect recovery data for FT#2 via `resume_from`. Check whether the Quest kit supports mid-rollout takeover. If not, record "recovery-start" demos that begin from typical failure states.
9. Data hygiene: never move the cameras, vary only object and cable poses, discard bad demos with B, and keep ≥50 demos per hard subtask. Use `base_share` 0.5 (keeps general skill) for FT#1, and consider 0.0 or cosine for a specialist FT#2.
10. Read `makermods-inspect-robots/docs/guide/scoring.md` before 8am. If organizers score with it, we can replicate their scorer locally and hill-climb against it.
